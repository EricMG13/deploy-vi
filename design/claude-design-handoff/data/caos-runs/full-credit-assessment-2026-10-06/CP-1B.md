## Analysis

### Credit view

Q2 2026 net revenue increased 3.0% year over year to $2,993 million, while Adjusted EBITDA fell 3.7% to $920 million; H1 revenue rose 2.8% to $5,863 million, while Adjusted EBITDA declined 1.7% to $1,807 million. [C3, C4] The shift reflects growth in Regional and Digital revenues offset by weaker Las Vegas performance and digital tax/hold pressures. [C14, C15, C16, C18, C19] Lower EBITDA against high debt and substantial lease cash requirements reduces the cushion available for interest, rent, capital spending and repayment. The strongest counterweight is positive revenue growth, H1 operating cash generation and reported covenant compliance; it would strengthen the credit signal if sustained alongside debt reduction and transparent headroom. Because later-period accounts, complete covenant calculations and discrete Q2 cash flows are unavailable, confidence is restricted and current leverage, FCF and covenant capacity are not upgraded by inference.

### Earnings, cash flow and credit transmission

**Earnings direction and quality.** Management attributes higher consolidated revenue mainly to higher casino revenues, including digital handle/hold growth; the company also identified Windsor consolidation, northern Nevada visitation and recent Tahoe/New Orleans investment as contributors, with Las Vegas leisure visitation and table-game declines offsetting part of the gains. [C17, C25, C33] H1 Regional Adjusted EBITDA increased to $923 million from $879 million, while Las Vegas fell to $836 million from $902 million; Digital increased to $137 million from $123 million in H1, despite Q2 Digital Adjusted EBITDA declining to $68 million from $80 million. [C14, C15, C16] The risk mechanic is uneven market performance: Regional growth and Digital handle are not a guaranteed substitute for Las Vegas earnings, and gaming hold and promotional/tax effects can create volatility. For creditors, that makes consolidated EBITDA durability and cash available for debt service less certain than revenue growth alone suggests.

**Cash flow and investment.** H1 operating cash flow was $675 million versus $680 million in H1 2025; property and equipment purchases were $335 million versus $453 million. [C5, C6] CP-1’s defined calculation register carries a $340 million H1 2026 CFO-less-PP&E-purchases proxy, not issuer-reported FCF. The proxy excludes other investing needs, debt principal, interest, taxes, distributions and financing-lease payments; it therefore does not establish cash available for debt reduction. The filing reports $368 million of H1 cash interest paid for debt and $686 million of cash interest paid for rent related to financing obligations. [C23, C32] With meaningful fixed cash claims, a renewed operating downturn or increased investment needs could consume liquidity before discretionary debt paydown; ongoing monitoring should track operating cash conversion after capex and rent rather than treating Adjusted EBITDA as liquidity.

**Leverage and liquidity.** CP-1’s canonical carrying debt is current debt plus balance-sheet long-term debt: $11,784 million at December 31, 2025 and $11,699 million at June 30, 2026. [C7, C8] Face-principal debt is a separate measure: the June debt note reports $11,807 million, and the balance sheet also presents $13,157 million of long-term financing obligations. [C10, C7] These claims must not be conflated. At June 30 the company reported $965 million cash and $2,928 million cash plus available borrowing capacity. [C9, C11] At FY2025, VICI/GLPI rent for 2026 was estimated at approximately $1.4 billion and subject to escalation. [C12] Liquidity plus revolver capacity provides a buffer, but draw capacity is not earnings or covenant headroom and the rent burden competes with debt service and maintenance needs. The filing reports covenant compliance as of June 30; the provided materials do not establish distance to covenant limits or available covenant capacity.

**Non-debt funding and earnings quality.** Customer deposits and other deferred revenue were $492 million at December 31, 2025 and $545 million at June 30, 2026. [C13] This operating float represents customer funds for gaming and future goods or services, not debt or ordinary payables. A demand shock, withdrawal or acceleration of service/redemption could convert the float into cash outflow; its timing and immediately refundable portion are not quantified, so it is not treated as unrestricted liquidity. Prior-year gains from the WSOP trademark and LINQ Promenade sales also affect comparison of transaction and other costs; reported Adjusted EBITDA remains the issuer-defined non-GAAP measure, not an analyst-normalized measure. [C28, C29]

**Downside path and trigger.** If Las Vegas visitation or regional demand weakens, the first operational break would be lower gaming volumes, hotel occupancy/rates and non-gaming spend; digital hold, promotions and tax changes can compound that pressure. Management’s Q2 discussion specifically identifies Las Vegas visitation and table-game weakness and elevated gaming taxes in certain states. [C18] Lower revenue/hold would reduce EBITDA and cash generation; capex, cash interest and lease rent would then limit retained cash, raising reliance on revolver capacity and refinancing. Observable triggers are sequential deterioration in segment EBITDA/hold and occupancy, operating cash flow falling below capex plus cash interest/rent, rising revolver borrowings, and a material decline in reported covenant cushion. No unsupported stress amount or covenant breach probability is inferred.

**Contrary evidence and conviction.** Revenue increased in H1, Regional and Digital H1 Adjusted EBITDA improved, operating cash flow remained close to the prior-year H1 amount, and the filing states covenant compliance at June 30. [C5, C14, C15] These facts would win if future filings show they persist, cash after capex and lease payments remains adequate, and debt falls without increased revolver reliance. They do not presently offset Las Vegas declines, lower consolidated Adjusted EBITDA, high fixed claims, unavailable covenant headroom and the missing post-June period. Accordingly the signal is mixed-to-negative for earnings durability, not a finding of near-term payment default.
