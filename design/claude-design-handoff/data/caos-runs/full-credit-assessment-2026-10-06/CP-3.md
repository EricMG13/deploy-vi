#### Credit and market synthesis

[C6, C7] The risk mechanic is weaker earnings conversion amid gaming hold, visitation and tax variability; this reduces cash available after debt service, rent and capex. H1 CFO was $675m, but it is not an issuer-reported FCF measure and does not establish cash available after all fixed claims. [C5] The balance-sheet carrying debt basis is $11.699bn (current plus long-term debt); the debt note separately shows $11.807bn face, $11.705bn book, and financing obligations of $13.157bn. Those figures have different roles and are not combined into one debt number. [C1, C4]

The strongest contrary case is the reported $2.928bn cash-and-capacity position at June 30 and covenant compliance at that date. [C5, C20] It would strengthen the credit view if subsequent statements showed stable liquidity, lower carrying debt and sustained cash conversion. It does not establish covenant headroom or October liquidity. The principal downside chain is: continued Las Vegas visitation/hold pressure → lower property contribution → reduced cash after rent, interest and necessary capex → greater revolver/refinancing dependence. [C8, C9, C12]

The two Caesars yields differ by 125.4878bp by arithmetic, but their coupons, maturities and call dates differ, and only the secured note’s first-priority status is supported by the delivered debt disclosure. The unsecured note’s seniority is supported as senior unsecured, but no common benchmark, spread, duration or executable liquidity data permits a clean relative-value premium.
