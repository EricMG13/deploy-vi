## Analysis

### Creditor implication

The notes covenant package gives creditors meaningful secured guarantees and restricts additional debt, liens, restricted payments and asset sales, but it is principally an incurrence-covenant structure rather than a maintenance test for the notes. The credit facility’s reported leverage and coverage covenants apply to the revolver and Term Loan A; their compliance does not establish noteholder headroom or test status for all term loans. [C1, C2] Ratio-based and fixed/grower baskets permit debt and value transfers subject to specified tests, while the actual usage and covenant EBITDA are unavailable. [C3, C4, C5, C6] Thus, the principal supported risk is potential incremental leverage and leakage under contractual baskets, not a demonstrated current breach or completed priming event. Monitor certifications, usage, amendments, subsidiary designations and collateral releases.

#### Controlling package and legal perimeter

The controlling note document is the executed February 6, 2024 indenture, as amended by the March 1 and August 23, 2024 supplemental indentures. Those supplements add guarantors, amend permitted-lien language and alter defeasance wording. The current credit agreement is not complete in the delivered set; fourth/fifth amendments are available, but they do not replace the base agreement. [C21, C22, C27] The FY2025 10-K says the secured notes and specified credit facilities are guaranteed by existing and future material wholly owned domestic subsidiaries; the 2032 notes’ guarantee is jointly and severally subject to a fraudulent-transfer limitation. [C16, C17] The current legal-entity, collateral and intercreditor schedules are missing, so reachability is described qualitatively only.

#### EBITDA, debt definition and ratio mechanics

The indenture uses defined EBITDA, leverage and Indebtedness concepts for ratio baskets. CP-1 reports FY2025 issuer Adjusted EBITDA of $3.624bn; CP-1D identifies recurring impairment and stock-compensation exclusions and unresolved transaction/other adjustments. These reported measures are not substituted for covenant EBITDA: the full definition, bridge and June covenant certificate are not available here. [C3, C4] Accordingly no legal leverage ratio or incremental ratio capacity is calculated. Balance-sheet carrying debt, face principal and the separately stated financing obligations are different measurement bases; CP-1 identifies June carrying debt of $11.699bn while the 10-Q debt note reports $11.807bn face principal and $13.157bn financing obligations. [C19] These financial observations do not establish the indenture’s legal debt numerator.

#### Debt, liens and MFN

The indenture permits debt under the credit-agreement basket subject to stated dollar and ratio conditions, and includes fixed/grower baskets, acquisition/refinancing routes, project/joint-venture debt and ratio capacity. One general basket is the greater of $750m and 32.5% of applicable-period EBITDA; the text also permits first-priority debt subject to a 4.50:1 senior-secured leverage test and other secured debt subject to a 4.75:1 total-secured test. [C3, C4] These provisions can allow additional leverage and, where permitted liens attach to collateral, dilute existing secured recoveries. The first supplement’s documentary-letter-of-credit inventory lien provision is limited to obligations connected with the relevant instrument and permitted debt. [C21] No outstanding basket usage or covenant EBITDA is provided; capacity is formula-only, not remaining availability.

The May and November 2024 credit amendments reduced Term B and B-1 pricing and contain a six-month 1% soft-call/repricing protection. The B-1 MFN described in the underlying agreement is narrowly conditioned by size, syndicated form, security rank, timing and maturity; the detailed current underlying agreement is not fully supplied. [C22, C23, C24] These are historical repricing terms, not a current estimate of repricing likelihood or remaining protection.

#### Restricted payments, investments and asset transfers

The indenture’s restricted-payment covenant has a builder/cumulative-credit route, specified baskets, and a leverage-based permission allowing a payment if pro forma consolidated leverage does not exceed 4.75:1. [C5] A separate general basket permits the greater of $500m and 22.5% of applicable-period EBITDA, subject to no continuing Event of Default. [C6] Investments in unrestricted subsidiaries have a separate cap of the greater of $210m and 10% of EBITDA, plus qualifying returns. [C7] Asset-sale proceeds can be reinvested or used for permitted purposes within 18 months, with offer thresholds and exceptions. [C8] These routes create theoretical value-transfer capacity from the restricted group; usage, current EBITDA, available builder credit and any completed transfers are not established. Consequently the review does not characterize the baskets as unused or aggregate them as independent capacity.

#### Collateral, guarantees and structural subordination

Secured notes rank equally with first-priority lien obligations and are secured under the security documents; the full collateral schedules and First Lien Intercreditor Agreement are not present. [C13, C16] Permitted releases include qualifying sales, unrestricted-subsidiary designations, guarantee releases and circumstances under the intercreditor/security documents. [C14] This can reduce collateral or guarantor reach when contractual conditions are met, but the supplied record does not show a specific material collateral release or quantify exposed value. The Caesars Virginia facilities are secured by Virginia-entity assets and are not guaranteed by CEI, a structural-priority distinction on those assets; entity-level value and debt allocation are not supplied. [C18]

#### Defaults, remedies and amendment exposure

The indenture’s amendment framework permits specified amendments without holder consent, including adding guarantors/collateral and non-adverse changes. Ordinary amendments generally require majority principal amount; specified adverse guarantee and collateral releases require a higher 66 2/3% threshold. [C10, C11] That creates meaningful sacred-right protection for specified matters, while leaving other changes subject to majority consent. The actual First Lien Intercreditor Agreement, standstill, turnover, enforcement-control and voting provisions are not available, so no class-level enforcement conclusion can be made. Covenant suspension is available following investment-grade ratings from at least two agencies and no continuing Default; reversion follows the stated ratings downgrade/withdrawal conditions. [C9]

#### Nearest pressure point

**Provision:** ratio debt and lien capacity under the indenture, including the 4.50:1 senior-secured and 4.75:1 total-secured ratio tests and fixed/grower baskets. **Risk mechanism:** if covenant EBITDA supports the tests, additional secured debt may raise leverage and share collateral; a downturn can simultaneously reduce EBITDA and weaken refinancing flexibility. **Creditor impact:** potential PD increase through leverage and debt-service burden, and LGD dilution if incremental debt shares collateral. **Monitoring trigger:** each compliance certificate, new-debt or lien notice, borrower amendment, and current covenant EBITDA bridge. **Evidence needed to tighten view:** complete current credit agreement, indenture definitions, current compliance certificate, basket tracker, collateral documents and intercreditor agreement. [C3, C4]

#### Overall legal-credit view

Overall, the documented package combines meaningful secured guarantees, collateral and sacred-right protections with material incurrence, restricted-payment and investment capacity. The annual report states the 2032 secured notes rank equally with first-priority lien obligations, and that material wholly owned domestic subsidiaries guarantee them; this supports a qualitative secured-credit position, not a quantified recovery estimate. [C16, C17] Weaknesses are the lack of a note-level maintenance test, capacity under debt/lien and leakage baskets, possible covenant suspension after qualifying ratings, and incomplete visibility into collateral, usage and enforcement terms. Current financial covenant compliance is a counterweight but does not prove headroom. [C1, C2, C9] No market comparator was provided, so market-norm characterization is not made. Overall aggressiveness is provisionally 4/5, weighted toward incurrence/leakage capacity and missing current capacity evidence; it is not a legal opinion or formal rating.
