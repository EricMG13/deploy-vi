## Analysis

### Downside view

Caesars’ earliest evidenced operating break is softening Las Vegas visitation and gaming performance: the Q2 filing reports lower Las Vegas segment revenue year over year, and table-games hold was weaker in both Q2 and H1 2026. Lower gaming and hotel contribution would compress cash generation while debt service, VICI/GLPI rent and planned capex remain material. That is the issuer-specific transmission toward weaker debt-service flexibility, slower deleveraging and greater refinancing sensitivity—not a forecast of covenant breach. The counterweight is meaningful: H1 revenue increased, operating cash flow remained positive and the company reported covenant compliance at June 30. Sustained operating improvement, debt reduction and timely transaction resolution would weaken the downside case; absent later reporting, current-condition confidence remains limited. [C3, C4, C5, C6, C13, C17, C18]

#### Downside transmission and counterargument

The initial break is not an assumed consolidated EBITDA decline: it is the reported weakness in Las Vegas revenue and table-games hold, against a portfolio where gaming is a principal revenue driver. Weaker visitation, table drop or hold can reduce casino and hotel contribution; labor, gaming taxes and other costs do not necessarily fall in step. The next conversion point is cash: H1 operating cash flow was $675m, while PP&E purchases were $335m, leaving a simple CFO-less-PP&E calculation of $340m before other investing, debt service, lease interest, distributions and other financing requirements. This is not issuer-reported FCF and does not establish cash available for repayment. [C3, C5, C18, C19]

The fixed-claim channel is substantial. At December 31, 2025 the company reported $11.9bn of indebtedness and $1.4bn of estimated 2026 VICI/GLPI rent; the June 30 update estimated $425m of debt service and $687m of lease payments for the remainder of 2026. These amounts have different dates and definitions and are not netted into a single ratio. [C6, C8, C9] A 100-basis-point Term SOFR increase was estimated to change annual interest cost by approximately $61m based on gross variable-rate borrowings at December 31, 2025; that is a dated sensitivity, not a current forecast. [C10]

The strongest contrary evidence is H1 revenue growth of 2.8%, $675m operating cash flow, reported $2,928m cash plus borrowing capacity, and covenant compliance at June 30. [C3, C5, C7, C13] It would prevail over the downside view if later periods showed sustained cash generation after capex and rent, stable or improving Las Vegas KPIs, declining debt, and maintained liquidity without increased facility reliance. The material qualification is that no post-June financials or current covenant calculation are in the delivered evidence. The pending merger agreement adds dated execution, approval and conduct-of-business risks, but its current status and financing progress after the Q2 filing are not established here. [C14, C15, C21, C22]

#### Catalyst view

The dated forward catalyst set is concentrated in the merger agreement’s conditional end-date and ticking-fee provisions. The initial merger end date is May 27, 2027, subject to conditional extensions to August 27, 2027 and November 27, 2027; the initial date for a potential ticking fee is June 26, 2027. The transaction’s credit effect is uncertain: completion could change ownership and financial policy, while delay, failure or required approvals could prolong uncertainty and transaction costs. The available filings do not establish the transaction’s status after Q2, the status of approvals or committed funding. Therefore probability is not quantified and calendar triggers are monitored as conditions-based events, not predicted closing dates. [C14, C15, C20, C21, C22]
