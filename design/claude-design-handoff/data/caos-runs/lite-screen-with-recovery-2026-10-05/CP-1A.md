## Analysis

### Analytical read-through

Caesars is a gaming and hospitality operator whose creditor profile combines casino and hotel cash generation, digital wagering, material property leases and substantial funded debt. The Q2 filing reports $2,993 million of quarterly revenue while consolidated Adjusted EBITDA was $920 million, down from $955 million; operating cash flow was $675 million for the first half. [C5, C6, C7] Results are uneven: Las Vegas Adjusted EBITDA declined, while Regional improved, and digital earnings remain exposed to wagering hold, promotions and gaming taxes. [C21, C22, C23] A proposed Fertitta transaction is signed but remains subject to shareholder and regulatory conditions; its funding is described in broad terms, not as a borrower-level sources-and-uses or pro forma leverage schedule. [C12, C14, C16] Current ownership percentages, lender reporting and transaction status after the reporting date are not fully established here. These limitations prevent committee-ready conclusions and make the transaction, leased-property cash burden and segment trends key downstream monitoring items.

**The snapshot below highlights reported facts, not calculated leverage or forecast values.** Debt presentations are not silently harmonized; the full carrying-value tie-out remains in the conflict register.

#### Key credit mechanics

The most immediate operating sensitivity is customer activity and gaming outcomes. The filing says results depend on customer volume and quality; in Las Vegas, lower leisure visitation, occupancy, hotel rates and table-game activity accompanied lower segment EBITDA, partly offset by slot handle. [C20, C21] If visitation or hold weakens further, revenue and segment earnings would be the first operational breaks; lower cash generation would then compete with debt service, property rent, maintenance and growth investment, potentially increasing revolver use and refinancing pressure. The supplied evidence does not support a quantified downside case. Monitor occupancy, table-game drop and hold, digital hold and promotional expense, gaming taxes, operating cash flow, capex and reported revolver availability.

The contrary evidence is meaningful: total revenue increased year on year in Q2, and Regional segment Adjusted EBITDA rose in both the quarter and six-month period. [C5, C22] That evidence would become more persuasive if sustained across periods and accompanied by stable consolidated EBITDA, cash conversion and lease-adjusted liquidity; it does not remove the Las Vegas decline or the need to meet debt, rent and capital requirements. [C6, C7]

**Funding and leverage perimeter.** The Q2 balance sheet lists current debt of $114 million and long-term debt of $11,585 million; the debt note separately presents $11,807 million face value and $11,705 million book value, while the earnings release gives $10,842 million of non-GAAP net debt. [C9, C10, C11] These are different stated bases. The detailed register logs them together; no leverage ratio is calculated from these conflicting presentations. The Q2 report also describes a $1.9 billion CEI revolver borrowing capacity, while its liquidity table includes cash and other facility amounts; a complete perimeter and availability reconciliation is not available in this evidence set.

**Transaction dependency.** The agreement provides for $31 per eligible share, with a ticking amount if closing has not occurred by the specified date; the issuer release separately values the transaction at approximately $17.6 billion including assumed debt of approximately $11.9 billion. [C13, C32] These are transaction disclosures, not completed financing or post-close issuer debt facts. The 8-K describes executed debt commitments, parent cash and equity as funding sources, but the supplied evidence does not allocate amounts by source or show pro forma borrower debt, EBITDA or liquidity. [C14] Shareholder approval, HSR and gaming approvals remain stated conditions. [C16]

### Governance view

Overall, Caesars’ sponsor/governance risk is **Insufficient Information** for a decision-useful classification. The record identifies Fertitta Gaming as the proposed parent if the merger closes and discloses a Carano-family-related holder with approximately 5% of shares agreeing to roll a portion of its equity; it does not establish the full current ownership register, post-close control rights, sponsor fund economics or completed ownership transition. [C12, C15] Documented capital-allocation facts include a $100 million 2025 common-stock repurchase, no repurchases in the first half of 2026, and a related-party property lease with annual rent of approximately $0.6 million. [C25, C26, C27, C28] These facts do not prove a creditor-adverse action. The strongest counterpoint is the filed Q2 reporting and disclosed transaction funding commitments; the missing complete transaction and current legal packages prevent resolving leverage, willingness, covenant usage and future creditor treatment. [C14, C29]
