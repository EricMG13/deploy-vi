## Analysis

### Liquidity view

At June 30, 2026, Caesars reported $965 million of cash and $1.9 billion of available CEI revolver capacity, while management expected liquidity and operating cash flows to fund operations and debt service for at least twelve months. [C17, C18, C23] That support is counterbalanced by $425 million of estimated remainder-2026 debt service and $687 million of VICI/GLPI lease payments; the disclosed cash-flow and capex figures are historical, not a forward FCF bridge. [C19, C20, C21, C22] The principal refinancing windows are the 2028 revolver/Term A maturities, 2029 unsecured notes and CVA facility, and the larger 2030 maturity cluster. [C1, C2, C3, C4, C6, C8, C12, C50, C51, C52, C53, C54, C57] Ordinary-course refinancing is the most credible conditional path, but no spread curve or forward funding-gap calculation is available. Legal-document flexibility is visible in the supplied 2024 conformed credit agreement; its current usage and current operative status are not verified. [C40, C49, C61]

**Maturity wall and basis.** The schedule groups annual principal maturities at $767 million in 2028, $1,574 million in 2029 and $3,962 million in 2030; the filing separately lists 2031 and 2032 instruments and $5,335 million thereafter. [C12] The 2028 CEI revolver and Term A balance mature January 31, 2028; 2029 includes the $1.2 billion unsecured notes due October 15 and the Caesars Virginia delayed-draw facility due April 26; 2030 includes Term B and the $2.0 billion secured notes. [C50, C51, C52, C53, C54, C57] The debt note gives face and book values, while the balance sheet reports current and long-term debt separately. The instrument schedule below uses carrying-value amounts, with the $6 million deferred revolving facility charge deducted from the revolver carrying amount; the reconciliation and gross-principal difference remain explicitly logged rather than treated as interchangeable bases. [C13, C14, C15, C16]

**Liquidity, FCF and market access.** Six-month operating cash flow was $675 million versus $680 million and property/equipment purchases were $335 million versus $453 million; neither comparison provides forecast FCF through the 2028–2030 maturities. [C19, C20] The filing reports $6.0 billion of long-term variable-rate borrowings and no cash-flow hedges, exposing debt service to rate movements; no sensitivity calculation is available. [C24, C25] FINRA last trades on October 1, observed October 2, show the secured 2032 notes at $93.28 / 8.063520% and unsecured 2032 notes at $84.94 / 9.318398%. [C26, C27, C28, C29] They are not bid, mid or evaluated prices, and the delivered records contain no spread, curve, quote-side, size or dealer evidence. [C30, C31] Issue-level ratings also differ and have different last-rated dates; they are not blended into a single market-access conclusion.

**Legal capacity and willingness.** The supplied conformed credit agreement contains incremental debt, extension, discounted prepayment, lien, subsidiary-designation and collateral-release mechanisms. [C40, C41, C43, C44, C45, C46] The 10-Q describes negative covenants that, subject to exceptions and baskets, limit debt incurrence, investments, restricted payments, liens and asset sales. [C42] Those provisions establish that certain routes are documented possibilities, not that current basket availability, voting outcomes, collateral coverage or remaining capacity is available. The current complete credit/lease package, executed current intercreditor evidence and CP-4A capacity calculations are missing. The CP-2C sponsor/governance handoff is not on this LITE route; no willingness, historical LME behavior or sponsor support is inferred. The proposed merger and its ordinary-course restrictions are context, not proof of creditor-directed action. [C38, C39]

**Strongest counterargument.** Reported cash, revolver availability, positive six-month operating cash flow, reported covenant compliance and management's stated twelve-month liquidity expectation support a less pressured ordinary-course case. [C17, C18, C19, C23, C36] If Las Vegas earnings stabilize, the liquidity expectation is borne out by a dated bridge, and refinancing markets remain accessible, vulnerability could move toward Low and consensual refinancing would be more credible. Conversely, further operating deterioration or revolver usage before the 2028 window would reduce that cushion and raise refinancing risk; no numerical downside case is asserted.

**Downside chain and monitoring posture.** A renewed decline in leisure visitation or gaming hold would first weaken Las Vegas revenue/EBITDA; lower cash generation, alongside interest, capex, rent and scheduled principal, could then increase revolver use and impair refinancing flexibility ahead of the 2028–2030 wall. The filing attributes Las Vegas weakness to lower visitation, occupancy and hotel rates and reports a year-on-year Q2 Adjusted EBITDA decline; Regional growth is a genuine offset, though partly associated with the Caesars Windsor consolidation and other drivers. [C33, C34, C35] Monitor segment EBITDA and cash conversion, CEI revolver availability, ratings/issue-level trading, and disclosed debt or lien activity. No numerical threshold beyond source-reported dates and figures is invented.
